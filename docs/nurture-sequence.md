# Nurture Sequence

The four-email sequence the [acquisition kit](acquisition-kit.md)
references: diagnosis, example, decision guide, audit invitation. For
leads who showed real intent but did not buy or book. Written to be
loaded into whichever email tool the owner picks; any tool that can
send plain text on a delay qualifies, and at current volume a calendar
reminder and a mail client qualify too.

## Who enters, who exits

Enter the sequence when one of these is true:

- An inquiry got the scope reply and went quiet for five business days.
- A fit call was held and the audit was proposed but not bought, and
  the loss reason was timing or no decision (never budget mismatch or
  capability mismatch; those are closed, not nurtured).
- An email was captured through the checklist offer, once an email
  tool and a capture form exist (owner-gated).

Exit immediately on any reply, booking, purchase, or unsubscribe. A
reply exits to a personal conversation, never to email five.

## Sending rules

- Plain text, from the founder's real address, replies welcome and
  read. No templates-pretending-to-be-personal: these are honest,
  useful broadcasts, and they read like it.
- One link per email. The link carries
  `?utm_source=email&utm_campaign=nurture` plus the email number so
  Plausible attributes the click without any personal data attached.
- Three to five business days between emails; the whole sequence lands
  inside three weeks.
- Every email carries a working unsubscribe line. Honoring it is not
  negotiable and neither is making it easy.
- Email three depends on the automation decision guide being published.
  If it is still a draft, hold at email two rather than substituting
  filler.

## Email 1: diagnosis

> Subject: The audit method, written out in full
>
> When we audit a WordPress operation, we do not start with the plugin
> list. We map how content, orders, and data actually move through the
> team, because that is where the cost hides. We published the whole
> method, checklist included:
>
> [How to Audit a WordPress Content Operation]
> (/insights/how-to-audit-a-wordpress-content-operation/)
>
> If your team can run it yourselves, you should. If you want it run
> for you, with access, accountability, and a plan at the end, that is
> what the paid audit is. Either way the method is the same.

## Email 2: example

> Subject: One WordPress engine, three sales surfaces
>
> A concrete example of what "WordPress as an operations platform"
> means in practice: one engine running web, point of sale, and a
> loyalty program for a working bakery, with the same products, stock,
> and customers everywhere.
>
> [The Pacífica case study](/work/pacifica/)
>
> The interesting part is not the stack. It is how much manual
> re-typing disappeared when the systems stopped being separate.

## Email 3: decision guide

> Subject: Plugin, Zapier, or custom code?
>
> Most WordPress automation questions are really this question, and
> the honest answer is "it depends on month twelve, not month one."
> We wrote up the decision sequence we use, including the cases where
> a US$99 plugin beats anything we would build:
>
> [A WordPress Automation Decision Guide]
> (/insights/wordpress-automation-decision-guide/)
>
> If you can answer the four questions in it, you know what to build.
> If you cannot, that is usually the audit talking.

## Email 4: audit invitation

> Subject: The fixed first step
>
> If the last three emails described your operation, here is the plain
> version of the offer. Ten business days. A workflow map, technical
> findings, opportunities ranked with the reasoning shown, and a
> 90-day implementation plan. Starts at US$2,500, scope and timeline
> fixed before payment, and you own the findings whether or not we
> implement them.
>
> [Request the audit](/wordpress-operations-audit/)
>
> Or reply with your biggest operational annoyance and I will send the
> exact scope I would recommend, including "this does not need an
> audit" when that is the truth.

## Measurement

Track per email: sends, clicks (Plausible, by campaign parameter),
replies, and exits by reason. Judge the sequence on replies and booked
calls, not opens. Revise copy after the first twenty completions, not
before; below that volume, every number is noise.
